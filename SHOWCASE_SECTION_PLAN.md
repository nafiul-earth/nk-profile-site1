## Part 1: The pattern you built in AI Images

Reading `aiShowcaseData.js`, the images section works because of five decisions. Everything below applies them to the other tabs.

**1. A project is a demonstrated capability, not a client.**
"One Face / Five Worlds" is not a customer. It is proof you can hold identity across scenes. "Object Study 01" is proof you understand form, shadow and negative space. The title names the skill.

**2. Every project carries a gallery of 4 to 8 pieces, each with `art` notes.**
The `composition`, `lighting` and `intent` fields are what separate you from a prompt account. Anyone can post an image. Almost nobody writes *"Treat lighting as narrative direction rather than decoration while holding identity and styling constant."* That sentence is the portfolio.

**3. Each gallery contains range, not repetition.**
Cold Current holds a sports drink, a blackberry wine, a porcelain coffee identity and a burger. Four categories, one heading. The viewer reads breadth and control at the same time.

**4. Categories use the words clients type.**
Fragrance campaign. Skincare system. Jewelry editorial. Children's illustration. Storybook sequence. YouTube design. These are search terms, not job titles.

**5. Every item is honestly labelled "Self-initiated concept."**
This is the quiet strength of the whole section. You show capability without implying clients you do not have.

### What went wrong in the other four tabs

They were written around the small number of projects that existed at the time. That produced items like *The Hidden Review Board* (patent process explainer), *Cloud, Without the Fog* (cloud service promo), *Maya the Inventor*, *Sarah the Reviewer* and *The Senior Inventor*.

Three of your six Character Design slots are corporate characters from one patent explainer. Two of your seven AI Video slots are B2B technical explainers.

**Nobody searches for those.** They are real work, but they are the narrowest possible slice of a market you are trying to enter broadly. The AI Images tab does not have this problem because you curated it later, around capability rather than around what happened to exist.

## Part 9: The gallery requirement

This is the part that matters most, and it is easy to skip.

**An item without an `art`-annotated gallery will look thinner than every item in the AI Images tab.** The images section works because each project opens into 4 to 8 pieces, each carrying composition, lighting and intent notes.

If AI Video ships as nine single videos with no supporting frames or notes, the tab will read as weaker than Images even if the films are good.

**For each video item, plan on:**
- the film itself
- 2 to 3 still frames as stills with `art` notes
- one storyboard or process image

**For each character item:**
- hero character image
- turnaround or consistency board
- expression sheet
- one in-context scene

**For each website item:**
- desktop screenshot
- mobile screenshot
- one detail crop

The notes are the differentiator. *"Treat lighting as narrative direction rather than decoration while holding identity and styling constant"* is the sentence that separates you from a prompt account, and it needs to appear in every tab, not just one.
